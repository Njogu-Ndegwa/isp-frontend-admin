Which hotspot packages sell the most in Kenya, and what should you charge? We looked at 84,804 hotspot payments made through Bitwave billing over 30 days to find out. KSh 10 is the clear winner: 1 in 3 customers buy it. KSh 5 comes second. But the cheap packages that sell most bring in the least money per sale, and that changes how you should set your hotspot package prices.

If you're building your first price list, here's what the payments say and how to turn it into a package menu.

## Which hotspot package sells the most in Kenya?

The KSh 10 package sells the most. Out of 84,804 hotspot payments, 1 in every 3 was a KSh 10 purchase. KSh 5 was second at 1 in 5, and KSh 20 was third at 1 in 8. Together those three prices make up about two out of every three hotspot purchases.

Here are the numbers from the video in one place:

| What we measured | Result |
|---|---|
| Payments analysed | 84,804 hotspot payments, last 30 days, Bitwave billing |
| #1 most bought price | KSh 10, 1 in 3 buys |
| #2 most bought price | KSh 5, 1 in 5 buys |
| #3 most bought price | KSh 20, 1 in 8 buys |
| Packages of 4 hours or less | 3 in 4 buys (75%) |
| Packages of 1 hour or less | 30% of buys, 12% of the money |
| Daily and weekly packages | 7 in 100 buys, 19% of the money |

Put simply: of every KSh 100 your hotspot earns, about KSh 19 comes from the few customers who buy a day or a week at a time.

## Why do short, cheap WiFi hotspot packages sell the most?

Short, cheap packages sell the most because that's how most customers buy internet: a little at a time, when they need it. Three out of every four purchases in the data were packages of 4 hours or less. Most people paying for hotspot WiFi in Kenya would rather spend KSh 10 now than commit KSh 50 or more upfront.

That matches what operators see on the ground. A customer at a stage, in a market or on a plot wants to watch a few videos, send some WhatsApp messages, and go. Your short packages are where the people are, so they have to be on the menu, and they should be the first thing customers see on your login page.

## Do the cheapest hotspot packages make the most money?

No. Packages of 1 hour or less made up 30% of all purchases but only 12% of the money. Daily and weekly packages were bought just 7 times in every 100 purchases, yet they brought in 19% of the money.

Work it out per sale and the gap is wide. Each percentage point of purchases in the 1-hour-or-less group earned 0.4 points of revenue (12 ÷ 30). Each point of daily and weekly purchases earned about 2.7 points (19 ÷ 7). So, on average, one daily or weekly sale brought in roughly seven times as much as one sale of a package lasting an hour or less.

There's a second benefit the table doesn't show. A customer on a weekly package pays you once and stays connected. A customer on short packages has to decide to pay again every time their time runs out, and some days they won't.

## How should you price your hotspot packages?

Build a ladder: keep cheap short packages for the crowd, then add daily and weekly packages for the customers who will pay more. The data says you need both. Drop the short packages and you lose most of your buyers. Leave out daily and weekly and you lose almost a fifth of the money.

A practical package menu has five rungs:

1. **An entry package at KSh 5.** The second-most bought price. It gets first-time customers to try your WiFi.
2. **Your main package at KSh 10.** One in three customers will buy this, so make it the most visible button on the page.
3. **A step-up at KSh 20.** The third most popular price, for customers who want a longer session.
4. **A daily package.** Fewer buyers, much more money per sale.
5. **A weekly package.** The best value for regular customers, and the most predictable income for you.

Which duration each price buys depends on your area, your competitors and your internet line, so we haven't put durations next to the prices. Check what other hotspots near you charge, then make sure each rung up the ladder is clearly better value than buying the cheaper rung several times. If a day costs the same as six KSh 10 packages, a regular customer has no reason to switch.

Then promote the longer packages. Put the weekly package on your login page with its value spelled out, and tell regular customers about it. ## How do you know which of your own packages sell?

Check your own sales by package every week, because your hotspot is not the average. A hostel full of students, a market and a residential plot will each buy differently. Bitwave's dashboard ranks your plans by revenue, so you can see which packages bring the money in and which ones nobody buys.

If a package hasn't sold for a month, replace it. If KSh 10 is doing all the work and daily packages barely move, try making the daily package better value for a few weeks and watch whether the money per day goes up. ## FAQ

**What is the most popular hotspot package price in Kenya?**

KSh 10. In 84,804 hotspot payments on Bitwave over 30 days, 1 in 3 customers bought the KSh 10 package. KSh 5 was second at 1 in 5 and KSh 20 third at 1 in 8.

**Should I remove my daily and weekly hotspot packages if they don't sell much?**

No. Daily and weekly packages were only 7 in 100 purchases, but they brought in 19% of the money. They sell less often, and each sale is worth much more than a short package.

**How many packages should a WiFi hotspot offer?**

Offer enough to cover both kinds of buyer: two or three cheap short packages (KSh 5, 10 and 20 were the top three prices) plus a daily and a weekly package. Too many options make the login page hard to read on a small phone.
