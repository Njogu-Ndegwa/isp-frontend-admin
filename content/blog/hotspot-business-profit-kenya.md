How much does a hotspot business make in Kenya? Honestly, less than TikTok makes it look, and it depends mostly on where you put it. One small hotspot we run in a market makes about KSh 800 a day. Over 30 days that came to about KSh 26,000 from 2,068 payments. Below are the real numbers from the dashboard, what comes off them, and what decides whether your site makes more or less.

You'll also see why the billing system matters to your income, not just to your setup. A hotspot on [Bitwave hotspot billing](/hotspot-billing-system-kenya) costs 3% of its hotspot revenue a month (KSh 500 minimum, no setup fee), so on a site like this one the billing bill is under KSh 800.

## How much does a small hotspot business make in Kenya?

A small hotspot in a busy spot with competition nearby can make around KSh 800 a day. Our own market site averaged KSh 862 a day over 30 days, a total of about KSh 26,000 for the month. That isn't a get-rich-quick number. You will not make KSh 100,000 a month from day one, and anyone who promises you that is lying.

Here's what the Bitwave dashboard showed for that site in the video:

The average payment of KSh 13 tells you what these customers buy: short, cheap packages. That's normal. Across 84,804 hotspot payments on Bitwave, 1 in 3 customers bought the KSh 10 package. ## What are the monthly costs of running a hotspot?

Your monthly costs are mainly your internet line, billing, and power. Billing on Bitwave is 3% of hotspot revenue: on KSh 26,000 that's KSh 780. The internet line is usually the biggest cost, and it depends on your provider and speed.

Here's a worked example using our starter-kit figure of about KSh 2,000 a month for a 10 Mbps line:

Your real figure will be different. A bigger internet line costs more each month. Electricity, a site owner who wants a cut, transport for repairs and replacement cables all come out of what's left. Write down your own line items before you trust anyone's profit figure, including ours.

The one-off cost of getting started is separate. Dennis said in the 800-a-day video that you can start from about KSh 30,000; our itemised minimum kit comes to about KSh 20,000. The full list is in our [hotspot business cost breakdown](/blog/hotspot-business-cost-kenya).

## Why does one hotspot make more than another?

Location decides most of your income. A spot with many people and few competitors will earn far more than a spot with few people, or a spot with many people and lots of competition, even if you spend exactly the same on equipment. Those two things, how many people and how many competitors, matter more than anything else you control.

Our market site shows this. Dennis's own words about it were that the numbers per client are "pretty terrible", because the area has high competition. It still makes money, but it's a lesson: before you buy anything, walk the area and count how many other hotspots customers can already connect to.

So when someone asks "how much does a hotspot business make?", the honest answer is another question: how many people will be within range, and how many other WiFi names will their phones already see?

## How does your billing system affect your income?

A bad billing system loses you customers. If a customer pays with M-Pesa and doesn't get connected, or the system keeps dropping people, they stop trusting your hotspot. The moment a competitor with a more reliable system shows up, those customers move.

That's why reliability is part of your revenue, not just a technical detail. With [M-Pesa WiFi billing](/mpesa-wifi-billing-kenya) that activates the package as soon as the payment confirms, the customer is online in seconds and never has to send you an M-Pesa message as proof. The fewer "nimelipa lakini sina net" calls you get, the more of those customers come back tomorrow.

Two other things eat into revenue quietly. If the internet or the router goes down in the evening, you lose your busiest hours; see [why hotspots slow down in the evening](/blog/hotspot-slow-evenings). And if people get online without paying, through tricks like HTTP Injector, that's money you never see; here's [how to stop the HTTP Injector bypass](/blog/hotspot-bypass-http-injector-mikrotik).

## Can you run a hotspot business without being on site?

Yes. Dennis runs the market site from home. On his phone he can see whether the router is online, how many customers are connected, who has paid, and which customers have a complaint, and he can deal with most problems from there. You only need to be on site for hardware: power, cables and the access point.

That matters for your income because it lets you run more than one site. A single small site at KSh 800 a day is a side income. Several sites, each in a good location, is a business.

## FAQ

**How much does a hotspot business make per day in Kenya?**

It depends on location, but one small Bitwave hotspot in a competitive market averaged about KSh 862 a day over 30 days, from roughly 69 payments a day at an average of KSh 13 each.

**How much profit does a hotspot make per month?**

Revenue minus your internet line, billing and power. On KSh 26,000 of revenue, Bitwave billing is KSh 780 (3%). With a KSh 2,000 internet line, about KSh 23,200 is left before electricity and your own time. Your internet cost may be higher.

**Is a hotspot business profitable in Kenya?**

It can be, if the location is right. A busy spot with few competitors earns far more than a quiet or crowded one for the same investment. It isn't a get-rich-quick business: our small market site makes about KSh 800 a day, and the way to grow is better locations and more sites.
